# The Aurelian League: Future Ideas

**Status:** Approved narrative design, not yet implemented  
**Created:** November 4, 2026  
**Purpose:** Record future story and gameplay ideas for the Pokémon interactive-fiction project. These notes detail the mutual-selection design recorded in [the story bible](docs/STORY_BIBLE.md), version 1.2. The current playable story ends before orientation; no starter-selection encounter is implemented yet.

## 1. Expanded First-Partner Selection

### Concept

In a future chapter after the four-o'clock League orientation in Lezano, the player visits the first-partner selection grounds. The field is a pleasant, open area with trees, flowers, a shallow pond, sheltered corners, and places for Pokémon to play. Contestants may meet and observe the Pokémon before choosing a partner.

There are **seven possible first partners**:

1. Charmander (Fire)
2. Chikorita (Grass)
3. Piplup (Water)
4. Scatterbug (Bug)
5. Sandile (Ground/Dark)
6. Tinkatink (Fairy/Steel)
7. Lillipup (Normal)

All seven are genuine, fully supported partner options, not decoys or inferior choices. The wider field may contain additional background Pokémon that cannot be selected as first partners. The seven choices are an intentional exception to the usual rule of presenting only two or three options at a time.

**Key idea:** The player does not simply pick a Pokémon. The player first has to demonstrate that the Pokémon is interested in choosing *them*.

### Exploration structure

- The player can approach the seven Pokémon in any order, visiting as many as desired before making a final decision.
- Each encounter starts with a brief observation that hints at the Pokémon's personality and priorities.
- Each encounter includes one meaningful decision with **two or three** responses, rather than seven choices within the encounter.
- A compatible response causes the Pokémon to show clear interest and makes it available as a partner.
- A clearly incompatible response causes that specific Pokémon to decline the partnership. The rejection remains in force **for this opening selection**, except for the explicit all-declined safeguard below; it does not imply that the player is cruel or a bad trainer.
- The player can still visit the other Pokémon after a rejection, and can return to inspect or select any Pokémon that has already accepted them.
- Players should be able to review which Pokémon are interested before confirming their final choice.
- Once a partner is confirmed, the remaining Pokémon become unavailable as first partners. Later encounters with them can still happen in the story.
- No species should require a particular player gender, personality stat, or preexisting relationship.

### Proposed encounters

These are plot beats, not final prose. Give each Pokémon an individual temperament instead of treating its species as a fixed personality template.

#### Charmander: Curiosity and patience

**Setting:** Beneath a tree, Charmander has arranged a small collection of stones and shiny objects. One object has rolled into a narrow gap in the roots, and Charmander is trying to retrieve it without upsetting its arrangement.

**Choices:**
1. **Help retrieve the object, then let Charmander put it back where it belongs.** **Accepted.** Charmander eagerly shows the player the collection and follows them a few steps.
2. Grab the whole collection and rearrange it to make things easier. **Rejected.** Charmander pulls the objects close and blocks the player from touching them again.
3. Sit nearby and watch until Charmander invites help. **Accepted.** This takes a little longer, but Charmander eventually brings an object over for the player to see.

**What this reveals:** Charmander is curious and sociable, but wants its interests respected.

#### Chikorita: Gentleness and attentiveness

**Setting:** Near a flower bed, Chikorita is quietly tending a patch of bent flowers, using its leaf to shade a wilted plant. Other contestants keep trying to call it away.

**Choices:**
1. Kneel nearby and help straighten the damaged flowers without disturbing Chikorita. **Accepted.** Chikorita relaxes and moves closer.
2. Offer Chikorita a treat to lure it away from the flowers. **Rejected.** Chikorita refuses to leave and turns its attention back to the plants.
3. Ask a caretaker whether the flowers need water, then help if permitted. **Accepted.** Chikorita watches carefully and nudges the player's hand afterward.

**What this reveals:** Chikorita notices whether someone pays attention to what matters to it.

#### Piplup: Respect and independence

**Setting:** At the pond, Piplup is practicing jumping from a low stone into the water. It slips on one attempt and looks around to see who noticed.

**Choices:**
1. Give it a little room and cheer when it succeeds on the next attempt. **Accepted.** Piplup pretends not to notice, then comes closer.
2. Scoop it up and fuss over it after the fall. **Rejected.** Piplup wriggles free, marches away, and refuses further attention.
3. Show that you can take an embarrassing moment in stride by laughing at your own minor slip or mistake, without laughing at Piplup. **Accepted.** Piplup seems relieved and approaches on its own terms.

**What this reveals:** Piplup wants a partner who respects its dignity without being overly serious.

#### Scatterbug: Care and observation

**Setting:** Scatterbug is nearly hidden among the leaves of a low shrub. A few larger Pokémon are playing nearby, making it difficult for Scatterbug to cross a narrow path to a patch of fresh leaves.

**Choices:**
1. Quietly ask the playing Pokémon to make some room and let Scatterbug cross at its own pace. **Accepted.** Scatterbug comes out of hiding and stays close.
2. Pick Scatterbug up and carry it across without warning. **Rejected.** Startled, it uses its defensive powder and retreats beneath the leaves.
3. Place an approved leaf snack close to Scatterbug, then back away and let it decide whether to approach. **Accepted.** It emerges cautiously and explores the player's hand.

**What this reveals:** A small, seemingly unremarkable Pokémon can form a strong bond with someone patient and observant.

#### Sandile: Play and boundaries

**Setting:** Sandile has buried itself in a warm patch of sand near the edge of the field. Only its eyes are visible. It keeps nudging a discarded ball toward nearby trainers, hoping someone will play.

**Choices:**
1. Roll the ball back and join its game while respecting its space. **Accepted.** Sandile pops out of the sand and starts another round.
2. Pull Sandile out by the tail to get a better look. **Rejected.** Sandile snaps defensively, retreats, and refuses to come closer.
3. Sit at a comfortable distance and make a little trail in the sand for Sandile to follow. **Accepted.** Sandile eagerly investigates and starts inventing new trails.

**What this reveals:** Sandile is playful and bold, but doesn't like being handled without invitation.

#### Tinkatink: Ingenuity and respect

**Setting:** Tinkatink is trying to repair a small toy or practice target using its handmade hammer. A piece keeps slipping out of place, and it is growing visibly frustrated.

**Choices:**
1. Hold the loose piece steady while Tinkatink does the hammering. **Accepted.** Tinkatink finishes the job and proudly shows off the result.
2. Take its hammer and finish the job yourself. **Rejected.** Tinkatink snatches the hammer back and refuses to cooperate.
3. Look around for a simple support or wedge Tinkatink could use, and offer it without taking over. **Accepted.** Tinkatink tests the idea and enthusiastically puts it to work.

**What this reveals:** Tinkatink likes a partner who takes its projects seriously and offers useful cooperation rather than control.

#### Lillipup: Reliability and trust

**Setting:** Lillipup is sitting beside a caretaker's basket with an important-looking cloth pouch tucked beneath one paw. It watches every passing visitor but refuses to leave its post.

**Choices:**
1. Ask the caretaker whether Lillipup is guarding something and offer to help without moving the pouch. **Accepted.** When relieved of its duty, Lillipup happily follows the player.
2. Offer a toy to distract Lillipup and slip the pouch away as a joke. **Rejected.** Lillipup barks sharply and stays with the caretaker.
3. Wait patiently with Lillipup until the caretaker returns, speaking to it while it keeps watch. **Accepted.** Lillipup wags its tail and stays beside the player afterward.

**What this reveals:** Lillipup values dependability and needs to know that a companion can be trusted.

### Important safeguards

**Prevent a no-partner dead end.** If the player rejects all seven through incompatible choices, the selection cannot simply strand them. Proposed solution: a caretaker intervenes and offers a final, explicit second-chance meeting with one Pokémon. The player may choose which Pokémon to revisit and receive a fresh, different opportunity to earn its trust. This exception is available only after all seven have declined; ordinarily, a declined Pokémon cannot be chosen. The player cannot finish the partner-selection sequence until at least one Pokémon willingly accepts. This exception should be presented as a compassionate fresh introduction, not a hidden reversal of the earlier rejection.

**Make outcomes understandable.** The Pokémon's response should show why it accepted or refused; never use an invisible compatibility score. If the player fails, the consequences should follow directly from the visible action. Avoid misleading dialogue choices where an innocent-sounding answer has an unexpected permanent penalty.

**Preserve agency.** The player can leave a successful encounter without committing, meet all seven Pokémon, and then choose from the ones that showed interest. Never auto-select the first Pokémon that accepts.

**Avoid mandatory combat.** No starter encounter should require winning a battle, having specialized knowledge, or using a particular item that the player might not possess.

**Equal story viability.** All seven should get companion-specific personality moments, noncombat utility, and later character development. No starter should block the eight-Laurel storyline or the Triumvirate.

**Keep the cozy tone.** The encounters should feel like meeting individual animals with preferences and boundaries, not seven exam questions. A refusal can sting briefly, but caretakers should respond gently and without shaming the player.

### Implementation sketch (engine-agnostic)

Suggested persistent state per species:

- `starter_<species>_status`: `unmet` | `interested` | `declined`
- `chosen_starter`: blank until final confirmation, then a species identifier
- `starter_nickname`: optional user-supplied name

For each Pokémon, provide a short introductory passage and a choice of two or three actions. Set that Pokémon's state based on the response, then return to the selection-field hub. The hub shows all seven species with their current state and allows revisiting the interested Pokémon to finalize the choice. Keep individual interactions short enough to avoid turning the opening chapter into a long checklist.

The caretaker's second-chance event is triggered only when **every** Pokémon has status `declined` and none is `interested` or `unmet`. Confirm selection explicitly before setting `chosen_starter` and leaving the area.

### Story-bible alignment

Version 1.2 of [the story bible](docs/STORY_BIBLE.md) records the same seven first-partner species, mutual-choice encounters, rejection behavior, all-declined safeguard, and confirmation flow. Earlier three-starter and unconditional-availability rules are superseded. This is an approved **future narrative feature** and does not imply that the current game implements starter selection.

## 2. Future development notes

- Give each chosen species a distinct response to the first League photographer's request to stage or repeat the moment of bonding. This can reinforce the League's media-production theme without revealing the larger mystery too early.
- Let the player encounter one or two unchosen Pokémon again later, perhaps with a new trainer or a caretaker, so the selection field remains part of the living world.
- Include occasional companion reactions to player decisions, but don't require seven independent versions of every passage. Use shared scenes with small, meaningful species-specific variations.
- Revisit evolution timing and noncombat abilities during the mechanics design phase, especially for Scatterbug, which evolves relatively early in the mainline games.

## 3. Remaining implementation details

- Each listed incompatible action causes a rejection for the current selection, subject only to the all-declined safeguard. Decide whether to include additional neutral, noncommittal interactions outside these meaningful choices.
- Exact wording and placement of the selection hub, accepted/rejected indicators, and final confirmation.
- Whether the caretaker's second-chance event is limited to the all-declined case (current proposal) or can be requested voluntarily.
- The amount of starter-specific content the author wants to maintain across later chapters.

## 4. Deferred visual assets: Pokémon sprites

**Status:** Future consideration only. Do not add sprite assets or alter the game UI yet.

- Explore existing video-game Pokémon sprites instead of generating Pokémon images when exact character proportions and consistency matter.
- Preferred visual starting point: the Pokémon Emerald (Generation III) sprite style, especially the compact pixel-art presentation. Emerald sprites cover species introduced in Generations I–III; later species, including Piplup, would need a compatible later-generation sprite or other authorized asset.
- Possible uses: character selection, the Pokémon party/status screen, Pokédex entries, and illustrative encounters.
- Compare available sets via the PokéAPI sprites repository and Sprite Finder, but do not assume that publicly accessible Pokémon art is free to republish. Review licensing, Pokémon intellectual-property rights, and distribution risks before selecting assets for a public release.
- If assets are ultimately approved for use, store them within a dedicated repository image/sprite directory and display pixel art with nearest-neighbor scaling. Avoid external image hotlinks in the published game.
- No new images or sprite assets are requested as part of these notes.
