# THE AURELIAN LEAGUE
## Pokémon Interactive Fiction: Story Bible
**Version 1.2 | November 4, 2026**

---

# 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

**Source of truth:** This document records approved narrative design. The playable repository content currently includes Chapter 1 (The Crossing) and Chapter 2 (Orientation), which ends *before* the four-o'clock orientation event. The seven-partner mutual-selection scene described below is approved for a later addition; it is **not yet playable**. Where planned scenes are described, they must not be mistaken for existing game content.

**Working Title:** The Aurelian League  
**Franchise:** Pokémon  
**Format:** Choice-based interactive fiction  
**Genre:** Adventure, mystery, character-driven drama  
**Setting:** Aurelia, an original Roman-inspired Pokémon region  
**Protagonist:** A customizable first-time Pokémon trainer in their twenties  
**Primary Objective:** Earn eight Laurels and qualify for the Triumvirate championship

### Core Premise

The Aurelian League is a newly established Pokémon competition in the Aurelia region. It is the first major League in which Pokémon battles are not required to advance.

Instead, contestants earn eight Laurels by demonstrating qualities such as compassion, resourcefulness, courage, wisdom, and cooperation with Pokémon. Challenges include solving mysteries, helping communities, exploring unfamiliar places, and dealing with difficult situations.

The League is also a major televised production. Cameras document contestants as they travel through Aurelia, and their adventures are broadcast to an enthusiastic audience.

What contestants do not fully understand is how extensively the competition is manipulated.

Some events are genuine. Others are carefully staged by the League's production staff. Actors may portray ordinary citizens, artificial problems may be created to test contestants, and seemingly spontaneous encounters may have been planned in advance.

Occasionally, a staged situation develops into something genuinely dangerous or emotionally consequential.

Beneath the competition's public spectacle, the League's leadership has secrets of its own. Certain officials manipulate contestants, influence the broadcasts, and conceal information that could threaten the institution.

The protagonist begins their journey seeking adventure, Pokémon companionship, and an opportunity to compete. Over time, they may become involved in increasingly significant mysteries surrounding the League.

The player's decisions influence friendships, Pokémon relationships, public reputation, discoveries, and ultimately the outcome of the competition.

### Desired Storytelling Style

The story should emphasize:

- Exploration and discovery.
- Pokémon with individual personalities and backstories.
- Friendships and meaningful relationships.
- Interesting mysteries with multiple possible resolutions.
- Choices that produce lasting consequences.
- A generally warm, adventurous atmosphere with occasional darker moments.
- A guided central plot rather than an unrestricted sandbox.
- Limited emphasis on Pokémon battles.

The protagonist should not begin the story as an investigator attempting to expose the League. The mysteries should emerge naturally through participation in the competition.

# 2. THE POKÉMON WORLD

### General Setting

The story takes place in an original region within the familiar Pokémon universe.

Existing Pokémon species, moves, abilities, and evolutionary lines are available. The world contains modern technology, Pokémon Centers, League-issued silver bracelets, transportation systems, research institutions, and established trainer culture. Traditional Poké Balls are not used in this setting; the bracelet is the means of forming and managing Pokémon partnerships.

Roman influences are primarily architectural, governmental, and cultural. The region is not a literal recreation of ancient Rome.

### Trainer Starting Ages

Unlike the traditional Pokémon games and anime, trainers in this setting normally begin independent Pokémon journeys between **18 and 22 years old**.

Most complete their general education before beginning their travels.

Some attend specialized trainer academies, while others work or pursue alternative education before becoming trainers.

Beginning a journey is not mandatory, and people may become trainers later in adulthood.

This allows the story to feature adult friendships, personal histories, relationships, and more complicated social situations.

### Pokémon in Society

Pokémon are integrated into everyday life.

Examples include:

- Grass-type Pokémon assisting with agriculture.
- Water-type Pokémon helping with canals and fishing.
- Flying-type Pokémon assisting with deliveries and transportation.
- Psychic-type Pokémon participating in research.
- Pokémon assisting with emergency services, construction, and conservation.

Not all Pokémon are trainers' companions. Wild Pokémon, household companions, working Pokémon, and Pokémon living independently all exist.

Individual Pokémon should have distinct personalities, preferences, relationships, and motivations.

Pokémon must be treated as characters rather than interchangeable tools for combat.

# 3. THE AURELIA REGION

**Status:** Working regional design. Aurelia is the confirmed regional name; most settlement names remain proposed rather than finalized.

Aurelia is a modern, prosperous peninsula inspired by Italy and the Mediterranean landscapes of the Roman world.

The region has a long history of civic institutions, public ceremonies, trade, infrastructure development, and partnerships between humans and Pokémon.

### Geography

Aurelia contains:

- A broad western coastline with major harbors.
- Fertile northern valleys and agricultural communities.
- Forested hills and old stone roads.
- Northern wetlands and extensive canal networks.
- Eastern mountains and historic quarry settlements.
- Southeastern volcanic highlands and geothermal springs.
- Southern coastal cliffs, islands, and sea caves.
- A central capital surrounded by developed countryside.

### Architecture

Roman architectural influences include:

- Amphitheaters and public arenas.
- Aqueducts and stone bridges.
- Public forums and civic squares.
- Colonnaded government buildings.
- Hillside villas and gardens.
- Ancient roads and surviving ruins.

These coexist with modern transportation, housing, technology, and Pokémon facilities.

### Transportation

The region has a functioning modern transportation network.

Major settlements are connected by railways, buses, and highways. Ferries serve coastal communities and offshore islands.

Ancient roads and rural trails remain popular among traveling trainers.

League contestants are encouraged to explore the region, particularly when challenges are designed around discovery, although public transportation remains available.

### Civic Culture

Aurelia has a longstanding tradition of recognizing citizens for contributions to the public good.

Historically, honors were awarded for courage, public service, protecting communities, and other significant accomplishments.

The Aurelian League claims to revive this tradition through its Laurel system.

Public reputation, civic pride, and patronage are important cultural influences.

Wealthy families and businesses frequently sponsor public projects. This has benefits but also creates opportunities for political favoritism, publicity-seeking, and corruption.

The region is a functioning society independent of the League. Its communities have legitimate economies, histories, disputes, and concerns.

# 4. REGIONAL LOCATIONS

Eight major destinations are currently planned as possible Laurel locations.

Their specific challenges and geographic positions remain working proposals.

## Lezano
**Location:** Western Coast  
**Role:** Starting location and first Laurel destination

Lezano is a busy coastal port with fishing boats, markets, harbor infrastructure, and extensive ferry connections.

It serves as the primary arrival point for new League contestants.

Important facilities include:

- Passenger ferry terminal.
- League Reception and Registration Center.
- Pokémon starter selection grounds.
- Pokémon Center.
- Waterfront markets.
- League media facilities.

The opening chapter takes place aboard a ferry traveling to Lezano, followed by arrival, registration, and starter selection.

The first Laurel may involve a harbor-related mystery or community problem.

**Confirmed name:** Lezano. It replaces the earlier name Port Lersano.

## Marnetto
**Location:** Northwestern Hills  
**Environment:** Orchards, vineyards, farmland

A rural agricultural settlement known for its irrigation systems and farming communities.

Grass-, Bug-, and Normal-type Pokémon are common.

**Potential Laurel storyline:** A failure in the local irrigation system threatens nearby farms. Investigation reveals suspicious damage or inconsistent explanations.

**Story function:** Introduces practical problem-solving, local relationships, and the possibility of staged challenges overlapping with genuine community problems.

## Miresto
**Location:** Northern Wetlands  
**Environment:** Marshes, canals, bridges, misty waterways

A settlement with longstanding traditions surrounding Ghost-type Pokémon and local folklore.

**Potential Laurel storyline:** Strange lights and unexplained sightings appear throughout the marshes. Some witnesses claim to have seen people who should not be there.

**Story function:** Introduces supernatural mysteries, unreliable testimony, and questions about what the League has staged.

## Cadreno
**Location:** Northeastern Plateau  
**Environment:** University buildings, museums, archives, historical sites

An academic center known for Pokémon research and archaeology.

**Potential Laurel storyline:** A disputed historical artifact leads to an investigation involving altered documents, missing records, and competing interpretations of regional history.

**Story function:** Introduces historical mysteries and the possibility of concealed discoveries involving the League.

## Vettane
**Location:** Eastern Highlands  
**Environment:** Mountains, quarries, old watchtowers, highland roads

A mountain settlement with extensive quarrying traditions.

Rock-, Ground-, and Fighting-type Pokémon are common.

**Potential Laurel storyline:** Researchers and their Pokémon become stranded after a mountain route closes. What begins as an organized rescue challenge develops unexpected complications.

**Story function:** Tests leadership, cooperation, responsibility, and difficult decision-making.

## Calterro
**Location:** Southeastern Highlands  
**Environment:** Geothermal fields, volcanic rock, hot springs

A resort settlement famous for hot springs and unusual geological formations.

Fire-, Ground-, and Rock-type Pokémon inhabit the surrounding region.

**Potential Laurel storyline:** Wild Pokémon begin abandoning an important geothermal habitat. Officials insist the changes are natural, but local caretakers disagree.

**Story function:** Introduces environmental concerns and possible consequences of the League's construction projects.

## Falcera
**Location:** Southern Coast  
**Environment:** Limestone cliffs, fishing villages, sea caves

A coastal community connected to marine Pokémon conservation.

Lapras and other aquatic Pokémon may travel through nearby waters.

**Potential Laurel storyline:** Changes in marine Pokémon migration patterns create problems for fishermen and wildlife researchers.

**Story function:** Explores Pokémon conservation, human economic interests, and conflicts involving League sponsors.

## Belloro
**Location:** Southwestern Hills  
**Environment:** Villas, gardens, estates, public squares

An affluent settlement known for wealthy patrons and large charitable projects.

**Potential Laurel storyline:** A highly publicized charity event involving a Pokémon shelter exposes conflicting accounts about finances and Pokémon welfare.

**Story function:** Explores the distinction between genuine generosity, publicity, and institutional corruption.

# 6. THE AURELIAN LEAGUE

**Official Name:** The Aurelian League  
**Television Program:** The Aurelian League  
**Championship:** The Triumvirate  
**Final Champion:** Aurelian Champion

The competition's official promotional slogan is provisionally:

**Eight Laurels. Three Finalists. One Champion.**

### Founding Concept

The Aurelian League distinguishes itself from traditional Pokémon Leagues by eliminating mandatory battling as a requirement for advancement.

Contestants are evaluated on their accomplishments, character, and relationships with Pokémon.

The inaugural competition has generated major public interest and attracted participants from across the Pokémon world.

### Entry Requirements

- Contestants must be eligible for independent Pokémon training.
- Participants may not bring personal Pokémon into the competition.
- Every contestant begins by selecting a new Pokémon partner from the League's approved starter program.
- The restriction applies even to experienced contestants who own Pokémon outside the competition.
- The official purpose is to create a relatively even starting point.

Contestants still possess different levels of personal knowledge, experience, resources, and social connections.

The League's fairness claims may therefore be questioned.

### Earning Laurels

Each contestant seeks to earn eight Laurels.

Laurels recognize accomplishments involving qualities such as:

- Compassion.
- Wisdom.
- Courage.
- Judgment.
- Loyalty.
- Resourcefulness.
- Cooperation.
- Leadership.

These are examples of qualities the League values, not necessarily eight fixed Laurel categories.

Challenges may involve helping people, assisting Pokémon, resolving disputes, investigating mysteries, exploring historic locations, or solving complex problems.

Each Laurel challenge should support multiple possible approaches.

Pokémon battles are permissible but never mandatory for League advancement.

### Rankings

Contestants receive rankings based on their accomplishments and overall performance.

Public evaluation criteria include:

- Laurels earned.
- Quality of accomplishments.
- Judgment and conduct.
- Compassion and public service.
- Courage and resourcefulness.
- Relationships and cooperation with Pokémon.

The precise ranking formula has not yet been established.

There may be opportunities for favoritism, subjective scoring, and manipulation by League officials.

### Qualification for the Triumvirate

Contestants must earn all eight Laurels before the season ends.

Of those who qualify, the **three highest-ranked contestants** advance to the championship.

Qualification is based on ranking, not on being the first three people to collect eight Laurels.

This allows contestants to take different approaches to completing challenges and creates competition around the quality of their accomplishments.

# 7. THE TRIUMVIRATE

The Triumvirate is the final championship event of the Aurelian League.

It brings together the three highest-ranked contestants who have earned eight Laurels.

The event is televised from Castrenne's Grand Amphitheater.

Unlike conventional Pokémon championship tournaments, the Triumvirate does not revolve around a series of mandatory Pokémon battles.

### Stage One: Trial of Wisdom

Tests the finalists' investigative abilities, reasoning, and judgment.

Possible components include:

- Solving a mystery.
- Evaluating conflicting evidence.
- Making difficult decisions.
- Identifying deception.

### Stage Two: Trial of Unity

Tests the relationship between trainers and their Pokémon.

Possible components include:

- Cooperative challenges.
- Rescue scenarios.
- Communication and trust.
- Solving problems through Pokémon abilities and teamwork.

### Stage Three: Trial of Triumph

The final challenge is not revealed to contestants in advance.

It determines the inaugural Aurelian Champion.

The precise nature of this trial remains undecided.

### Hidden Championship Conflict

Certain members of League leadership have a preferred contestant whom they want to win.

They may manipulate rankings, publicity, opportunities, or events to favor this individual.

The League as a whole is not necessarily involved in the manipulation.

The protagonist may learn about the intended winner before the championship.

The player's discoveries, relationships, and previous decisions should affect how the final event unfolds.

# 8. THE TELEVISION PRODUCTION

The entire Aurelian League is a major televised entertainment production.

Contestants understand that they are being filmed and that some League challenges are organized for entertainment.

They do not fully understand the extent of the production's manipulation.

### Production Methods

The League may employ:

- Camera crews accompanying contestants.
- Hidden cameras in designated competition areas.
- Interviews and confessionals.
- Edited episode narratives.
- Planned encounters.
- Professional actors.
- Staged disputes and mysteries.
- Manufactured rivalries.
- Selective presentation of contestant behavior.

The program is interested in creating compelling television, not necessarily documenting every event objectively.

### Three Types of Events

**Genuine events:** Real people, Pokémon, and communities experience actual problems requiring assistance.

**Staged events:** Production staff arrange scenarios to evaluate contestants or create entertainment.

**Mixed events:** A staged scenario becomes genuinely consequential, or a legitimate problem is manipulated for television.

### Central Uncertainty

Contestants cannot always identify which events are authentic.

Possible questions include:

- Is a person genuinely asking for help?
- Is an argument real or scripted?
- Is a rival acting naturally or following production instructions?
- Was a mysterious encounter deliberately arranged?
- Is a Pokémon genuinely frightened or participating in a supervised challenge?
- Has footage been edited to create a misleading impression?

Importantly, staged encounters can still produce genuine friendships, emotions, and consequences.

Not everything should be staged. Ordinary life in Aurelia must continue independently of the show.

# 9. LEAGUE ORGANIZATION

The League has several departments with potentially competing interests.

### Competition Office

Administers Laurels, contestant evaluations, rankings, and rules.

Potential conflict: Favoritism and inconsistent judgment.

### Production Office

Coordinates filming, interviews, broadcasts, and entertainment.

Potential conflict: Manipulation of contestants and events for ratings.

### Civic Affairs

Works with local governments and communities to arrange challenges.

Potential conflict: Pressure to turn legitimate problems into entertainment.

### Pokémon Welfare

Oversees Pokémon safety and treatment.

Potential conflict: Pressure to approve activities that may not be in a Pokémon's best interests.

### Historical Commission

Works with historic sites, archaeological discoveries, and preservation projects.

Potential conflict: Concealing historical information that could disrupt League development.

### Institutional Morality

The Aurelian League should not be portrayed as entirely evil.

Many employees genuinely believe in its mission.

Some manipulate events for harmless entertainment, some act from greed or ambition, and others may participate in more serious wrongdoing.

Characters should have distinct personal motivations.

# 10. THE MAIN MYSTERY

**Status:** General mystery framework established. Specific underlying truth remains a working proposal.

### Central Themes

The major narrative themes include:

- Appearance versus reality.
- Performance versus sincerity.
- Manufactured heroes and public reputation.
- Institutional power.
- Truth and deception.
- The value of genuine friendship.
- Pokémon welfare.
- The difference between public recognition and personal integrity.

### Established Mystery Elements

The League's public presentation is not entirely truthful.

Certain contestants receive favorable treatment.

Some televised encounters are manipulated.

Production staff may conceal or distort events.

Some officials possess information they do not want the public to discover.

A preferred contestant is being positioned for victory.

### Working Historical Mystery

Aurelia once maintained protected travel corridors used by wild Pokémon.

These corridors followed natural waterways, ancient roads, tunnels, and mountain passages.

Over generations, some were abandoned or forgotten.

The League's recent construction and restoration projects may have damaged these routes or disturbed important Pokémon habitats.

Some officials discovered this problem during development but suppressed the information to prevent delays to the competition.

This could explain apparently unrelated disturbances across the region, including:

- Changes in Pokémon migration.
- Ghost-type activity around old structures.
- Unusual behavior near archaeological sites.
- Environmental problems.
- Missing or altered historical records.

This is a proposed underlying mystery, not yet finalized.

### Mystery Design Principles

Not every smaller mystery should connect to the central conspiracy.

Some should be ordinary personal conflicts, genuine supernatural encounters, or harmless staged challenges.

Some mysteries should have several valid resolutions.

The protagonist should uncover information gradually through relationships, investigations, and exploration.

Investigating the League should be possible but should not be mandatory for enjoying the main competition.

# 12. STARTER POKÉMON

### Starter Design Rules

Starter Pokémon have individual personalities, not rigid species stereotypes.

The player's choice should influence:

- Companion dialogue.
- Relationship development.
- Exploration scenes.
- Interactions with other Pokémon.
- Opportunities to solve problems.

No species should offer an exclusive solution required to finish a Laurel or the Triumvirate. The first partner's personality can affect the *approach* and later dialogue, but all seven have equivalent narrative importance.

The player may nickname the starter.

Evolution should be treated as a meaningful part of character development rather than simply a mechanical reward for battling.

# 13. ACQUIRING ADDITIONAL POKÉMON

The protagonist may eventually obtain more Pokémon companions.

Possible methods include:

- Traditional capture involving battles.
- Befriending wild Pokémon.
- Helping injured or distressed Pokémon.
- Receiving Pokémon as League rewards.
- Being entrusted with Pokémon by other characters.
- Forming partnerships with Pokémon involved in mysteries.
- Meeting Pokémon who voluntarily wish to travel with the protagonist.

Battles can occur, particularly during certain captures, but are not the primary mechanism of progression.

Pokémon companionship should feel personal. The League's entrant guide makes consent an ongoing part of that companionship: a Pokémon must choose to join its trainer, and any Pokémon on a team may leave if its trainer mistreats it, even after the partnership is established. This is an approved story rule, not yet an implemented automatic party-departure mechanic. Future scenes should distinguish genuine mistreatment from ordinary disagreements and give clear narrative context if a Pokémon chooses to leave.

New Pokémon should have distinct personalities and, when appropriate, individual character arcs.

Each League bracelet can hold a team of up to six Pokémon, and only one of a trainer's Pokémon may be out of the bracelet at a time. A Pokémon joins by willingly touching the trainer's bracelet; it must agree to the partnership and can leave if mistreated. There are no Poké Balls or separate storage boxes. The engine currently tracks a party of up to six, but bracelet storage and active-partner switching are narrative rules awaiting dedicated gameplay mechanics.

# 15. OPENING SEQUENCE: FERRY, LEZANO, AND FIRST PARTNER

**Current playable chapters:** Chapter 1, *The Crossing*, and Chapter 2, *Orientation* (which currently ends before the orientation event begins). The partner-selection field is a **future planned scene**, after orientation, not a completed chapter-one event.

The opening sequence should emphasize anticipation, meeting other contestants, and eventually choosing the protagonist's first Pokémon.

No major conspiracy should be revealed immediately.

## Scene One: The Ferry

The story begins aboard a passenger ferry carrying a large group of prospective Aurelian League contestants toward Lezano.

Exactly one hundred contestants were accepted for the inaugural season. The ferry, the Portia, carries contestants, crew members, and League press, not ordinary holiday passengers.

Most are young adults beginning their first journeys.

Contestants have not brought personally owned Pokémon, since the League prohibits doing so. Crew Pokémon, including Minccino and Machoke, may still be present and working aboard.

### Establishing Details

- Crowded decks and passenger lounges.
- Excited conversations about Pokémon.
- Speculation about the new League.
- Information about Laurels and rankings.
- The approaching coastline of Aurelia.
- The absence of contestants' own Pokémon, contrasted with the crew's working Pokémon.

### Possible Player Activities

The protagonist may:

- Visit the observation deck.
- Speak with other contestants.
- Review League information.
- Explore the passenger lounge.
- Spend time alone preparing for the journey.

Several activities may be completed before docking.

These choices establish initial personality traits and relationships.

## Scene Two: Meeting Other Contestants

The protagonist has opportunities to interact with the three proposed recurring contestants.

Conversations can introduce:

- Motivations for entering.
- Opinions about the League.
- Expectations about starter Pokémon.
- Views about competition and cooperation.
- Information about the Triumvirate.

The player may be friendly, skeptical, competitive, reserved, or humorous.

Friendships should not be forced.

Different choices influence later relationships and dialogue.

## Scene Three: Arrival in Lezano

The ferry docks at Lezano.

The harbor is crowded with:

- League officials.
- Contestants.
- Reporters.
- Television crews.
- Spectators.
- Local residents.

Large red-and-gold banners advertise the competition.

**Eight Laurels. Three Finalists. One Champion.**

The protagonist proceeds toward registration.

### Reporter Encounter

A reporter from the League's broadcast network may approach the protagonist for an interview.

The player can:

- Agree enthusiastically.
- Answer cautiously.
- Give confident or humorous responses.
- Decline politely.

Potential interview questions include:

- Why did you enter the Aurelian League?
- What do you hope to accomplish?
- Do you believe you can become Champion?
- What makes someone a good Pokémon trainer?
- What kind of Pokémon partner are you hoping to find?

Responses may influence the protagonist's early public image.

They may also appear in later edited broadcasts.

The reporter should initially seem like an ordinary professional, not an obvious antagonist.

## Scene Four: Registration

The protagonist reports to the League Reception and Registration Center.

Registration includes:

- Confirming personal information.
- Receiving official contestant identification.
- Learning basic League rules.
- Receiving introductory information about Laurels.
- Learning how to obtain a starter Pokémon.

Staff explain that contestants will meet their prospective first partners after the scheduled orientation. The existing playable content currently stops before that event.

## Scene Five: The Starter Field

The planned partner-selection grounds are a large open area near the registration facilities, visited following orientation, where eligible Pokémon live and interact.

The field contains:

- Grass and shaded trees.
- Flower beds.
- A pond.
- Shelters.
- Play areas.
- Open spaces for Pokémon interaction.

Many species of Pokémon are present.

Possible background species include:

- Eevee.
- Vulpix.
- Growlithe.
- Shinx.
- Sandshrew.
- Other familiar Pokémon.

Prospective trainers interact with Pokémon throughout the field.

Some Pokémon are playing, resting, exploring, or approaching interested humans.

### The Seven Selectable Pokémon

The seven possible first partners are **Charmander, Chikorita, Piplup, Scatterbug, Sandile, Tinkatink, and Lillipup**. They are available as full companions, even if other Pokémon in the field are background residents. The seven-entry selection hub is an intentional exception to the normal two- or three-choice menu preference.

Each candidate has a distinct situation that reveals what matters to it:

- **Charmander:** Arranging a collection of stones and shiny objects under a tree, with one piece stuck among the roots. It appreciates patient help and respect for its belongings.
- **Chikorita:** Tending drooping flowers near a bed of wildflowers. It responds to gentle attention and help with its task, not attempts to lure it away.
- **Piplup:** Practicing jumps at the pond and feeling embarrassed by a slip. It values encouragement that respects its dignity and independence.
- **Scatterbug:** Hesitant to cross a path where larger Pokémon are playing. It feels comfortable when the player makes space without grabbing it.
- **Sandile:** Hiding in warm sand and nudging a ball toward passersby. It enjoys playful participation while maintaining boundaries.
- **Tinkatink:** Struggling to fix a small toy or practice target using its hammer. It welcomes cooperation, not someone taking over.
- **Lillipup:** Guarding a caretaker's pouch. It warms to someone who respects its responsibility and waits or offers appropriate help.

### Mutual-Choice Encounters

The player can approach the seven Pokémon **in any order**, meet all of them, and return to the selection hub between encounters. Each individual encounter offers **two or three clearly differentiated responses**:

- A considerate response earns that Pokémon's interest; its status becomes `interested`.
- An incompatible action causes a visible, understandable refusal; its status becomes `declined` and it cannot normally be chosen during this first-partner selection.
- A refusal reflects that individual Pokémon's preferences, not a moral judgment of the protagonist.

No hidden personality check, combat result, special item, or gender restriction controls acceptance. Feedback must make the Pokémon's response intelligible. A player may meet other candidates or choose among the Pokémon already marked `interested`, rather than being forced to take the first one that accepts.

**All-declined safeguard:** If and only if all seven candidates decline, a caretaker offers one compassionate, explicitly different second-chance introduction to a Pokémon the player chooses to revisit. The player must still earn its trust, and cannot be stranded with no partner. This is an exception to the normal rejection rule, not permission to retry declined Pokémon freely.

### Final Partner Selection

When ready, the player chooses **one interested Pokémon** and explicitly confirms the partnership. A caretaker verifies that it has willingly chosen the trainer, guides it to touch the trainer's silver League bracelet, and offers an optional nickname. The remaining Pokémon cannot be selected as first partners, though they may return in later story scenes.

The detailed choice prompts and suggested state variables are in [FUTURE_IDEAS.md](../../FUTURE_IDEAS.md). No selection implementation has yet been added to the playable story.

- Local residents.

- Rebellious.
- Entertaining.

Tracks the administration's opinion of the protagonist.

Possible influences include:

Tracks:

- Trust.

### Choice Design Principles

Player decisions should affect future content.

Examples include:

- A befriended Pokémon returning to help.
- A contestant becoming an ally or rival.
- A missed clue changing an investigation.
- A damaged relationship restricting cooperation.

- Evidence changing the championship's outcome.

However, the story should remain manageable to write.

Use persistent variables, narrative callbacks, and meaningful branches without requiring entirely separate plots after every decision.

Choices should not merely offer cosmetic dialogue differences.

# 18. IMPORTANT STORY DESIGN RULES

1. **Pokémon battles are not mandatory for League progress.** Battles may occur naturally during exploration, encounters, or captures.

2. **The protagonist begins as an ordinary new trainer.** They should not immediately become a detective or conspiracy investigator.

3. **Not every encounter is staged.** Aurelia must feel like a genuine region filled with real people and Pokémon.

4. **Staged encounters can have real consequences.** An actor may become a genuine friend, or a planned challenge may develop into a real emergency.

5. **The League is not entirely evil.** Institutional corruption should involve believable personal motivations and conflicting interests.

6. **Pokémon relationships matter.** Companions should have individual personalities and ongoing development.

7. **The story remains guided.** The player should have meaningful choices without needing to navigate an unrestricted sandbox.

8. **Mysteries should have satisfying resolutions.** Not every problem must connect to the central plot.

9. **Multiple approaches should be valid.** Compassion, reasoning, persistence, negotiation, and cooperation may all produce successful outcomes.

10. **The player's earlier choices must matter during the finale.** Relationships, evidence, and reputation should influence the Triumvirate.

11. **The protagonist's personality is player-defined.** Avoid assuming the player is automatically kind, suspicious, heroic, or ambitious.

12. **The opening should prioritize excitement and companionship.** Introduce darker elements gradually rather than immediately revealing the League's secrets.

# FINAL CREATIVE DIRECTION

The Aurelian League should feel like a genuine opportunity for an ordinary young adult to begin a wonderful Pokémon adventure.

The protagonist travels through an unfamiliar region, develops friendships, discovers interesting Pokémon, and works toward an exciting championship.

At the same time, the League's unusual structure creates opportunities for deception, manipulation, ethical dilemmas, and mysteries.

The central question is not simply whether the protagonist can win.

It is what kind of trainer they become, what they discover about the institution they're participating in, and how their choices affect the people and Pokémon they encounter.

**The ideal experience combines Pokémon companionship, regional exploration, branching mysteries, and the spectacle of a televised competition in which appearances cannot always be trusted.**
