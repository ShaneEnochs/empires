# The Aurelian League: Golden Path Outline

**Working outline | November 5, 2026 | Narrative v2.0**

**Status:** This is a scene-level **working plan**, not playable content and not a final script. For established setting and transformation rules, see [STORY_BIBLE.md](STORY_BIBLE.md). Scenes may be split, combined, rearranged, or revised during authoring. These are the principal beats in a successful playthrough, not the only choices or outcomes.

**Core assumptions:** The protagonist is a junior **Aurelian League field warden** and does not know that Team Rocket controls the League. The League is midway through its first competitive season. The player rescues **Charmander, Chikorita, or Piplup** from a cargo ship; **whichever species is chosen has the same former human identity**. The protagonist begins their new life without a tracked money balance. Food, lodging, basic supplies, and Poké Balls come through odd jobs, hospitality, and authored events rather than a credit economy.

## Act I: The Defection

1. **A Simple Assignment.** The protagonist and **Cassius**, an experienced League field warden, repeatedly fail to catch a **Chikorita** in the grass. The capture eventually succeeds; responses can establish approach and temperament. **Hidden conditional history:** if the player later rescues Chikorita, this is **Darrow**, who escaped the facility using **Vine Whip to manipulate a lock**. If the player rescues Charmander or Piplup, Darrow **never escaped** and the opening Chikorita is a **different transformed prisoner**. Nothing in this opening scene reveals that distinction.
2. **Back to Work.** On the return trip, Cassius discusses field-welfare assignments and the League's inaugural season, now roughly halfway completed. The protagonist believes the work is legitimate.
3. **The Cargo Ship.** Both wardens reach the dock. An attendant chases an escaped Pokémon; Cassius joins the chase and tells the protagonist to leave their delivery on deck. A sign reading **"Pokémon to Holding Area"** persuades the protagonist to take it below.
4. **Belowdecks.** The protagonist discovers imprisoned Pokémon and clues that the League's relocation claims are false. **Internal reflection choices** express disbelief, fear, anger, guilt, or suspicion without foreclosing the rescue.
5. **The First Rescue.** The player chooses **Charmander, Chikorita, or Piplup** to release first. The Chikorita is the one they delivered, while the chosen species is always **Darrow** in this playthrough. In the Chikorita route the protagonist therefore frees the very Pokémon they helped capture. Someone approaches or enters before more cages can be opened. The protagonist escapes with only that Pokémon.
6. **Nowhere to Go.** Having abandoned their League assignment, the protagonist flees the harbor with few possessions and no reliable income. The immediate goal is safety and shelter.

## Act II: Starting Over

7. **The Barn.** The protagonist hides in a barn beyond Lezano. An older, rough-edged farmer discovers the trespasser and is initially angry. Conversation about hardship lets the player decide how honestly to explain their circumstances.
8. **An Honest Day's Work.** The farmer offers or arranges practical work in exchange for a meal and a place to stay. The scene builds trust through actions rather than requiring a wage or credit calculation.
9. **Getting Acquainted.** The protagonist learns about the starter's needs and disposition. Its above-average intelligence surfaces as something endearing or curious, not yet a clear plot revelation.
10. **A Second Opportunity.** An odd job in or near **Marletto** introduces more townspeople and ordinary wild Pokémon. Basic supplies or Poké Balls may be given in thanks; a new Pokémon encounter is possible.
11. **Someone Is Missing.** In Marletto, the chosen starter becomes unusually affectionate toward **a widowed woman and her children**, regardless of starter species. Her husband died, and her **older brother moved in to help raise the children**. He later went out looking for the family's missing **Herdier** and never returned. **Herdier found its way home alone**, but the brother remained missing. The brother is **Darrow, 32**, a kindhearted but stubborn odd-job worker. While searching, he wandered too close to a restricted League area and was **surrounded and abducted by Rocket operatives**. They took him to a facility, used **Polymorph** to transform him, and he **escaped**, though he eventually ended up back in captivity on the cargo ship: **the starter is her missing brother**. The family's Herdier, which usually dislikes other Pokémon, becomes unexpectedly excited and affectionate toward the starter, as though it knows him. She tells the protagonist she has never seen a Pokémon of that species before, and nobody understands Herdier's behavior. When it is time to leave, the companion is reluctant to part from the family and Herdier but eventually agrees to follow the protagonist. The connection is not revealed to the player yet.
12. **An Old Acquaintance.** **Cassius** seeks out the protagonist because the junior employee's disappearance has endangered his unusually well-paid League job, which supports his **five children**. He has long suspected something is wrong but deliberately avoided looking further. He is basically decent and personally trustworthy but morally compromised and of limited immediate help.

## Act III: The Invitation

13. **An Unexpected Visitor.** A representative of an independent research organization finds the protagonist and says the rescued Pokémon may help bring down those running the League. The representative refuses to explain the deeper suspicion without an examination.
14. **The Pokémon's Decision.** The representative asks to take the Pokémon directly to an institute. It refuses to leave the protagonist. The representative accepts this, provides the destination, and promises to misdirect the pursuers.
15. **Preparing to Leave.** People met through odd jobs may assist with shelter, provisions, and transportation. The protagonist plans the journey without a money-management system.
16. **On the Road.** A travel problem or Pokémon encounter shows the companion's unusual intelligence or developing power, while creating space for ordinary Pokémon adventure and optional character moments.
17. **The Crossing.** The protagonist reaches a means of leaving Rocket-influenced Aurelia and faces a final danger or obstacle before arriving near the independent institute.

## Act IV: The Truth

18. **The Research Institute.** Researchers meet the protagonist and companion. Initial findings reveal something exceptional, though the full meaning remains unclear.
19. **The Missing People.** The institute explains its investigation into disappearances, League-controlled transportation, and suspicious Pokémon shipments. The protagonist connects these findings to assignments they carried out.
20. **What You Used to Be.** The institute discovers Rocket's use of **Polymorph**, an unusual Ditto's reverse-Transform ability. Rocket turns abducted people into specified Pokémon species with essentially perfect stats for black-market buyers. **Mixed human/Pokémon DNA in victims not yet permanently changed** provides evidence of the process. A **strangely shaped birthmark matching Darrow's human birthmark** is noticed on the starter during later examination; records or other testimony are needed to corroborate the match after his transformation becomes permanent. The selected starter is **Darrow** in all three species routes.
21. **Too Late.** Researchers determine that the starter's transformation can no longer be reversed. The earlier affectionate encounter and reluctance to leave the missing person's loved one acquire their full meaning.
22. **A Chance to Stop It.** Other victims may still be within a reversible window. The institute needs additional evidence and help reaching the facilities or imminent shipment. The protagonist chooses to help.

## Act V: Making Amends

23. **Back to Aurelia.** The protagonist returns and reconnects with people met earlier. Established relationships shape what information and shelter are available.
24. **The Inside Man.** Cassius faces the consequences of having looked away. Depending on their relationship and his choices, **he may help the protagonist gain access to a guarded facility** and assist in bringing the operation down.
25. **The Laboratory.** The protagonists and allies find evidence of the experiment, other transformed victims, and the unusual Ditto being exploited by Rocket.
26. **The Rescue.** The protagonist attempts to free victims and stop a shipment or expansion of the technology. Earlier alliances and decisions affect available approaches and the number of people saved.
27. **The End of the Operation.** The operation is exposed and stopped in the golden-path outcome. The Ditto and surviving subjects can be recovered, but different decisions may affect particular outcomes.
28. **A Different Life.** The starter remains a Pokémon. The protagonist, their companion, and their friends face what remains to be rebuilt; the final scene reflects the companion's present-day autonomy and the life they have chosen together.

## Mystery design: work backward from the answer

The mystery is more reliable if the truth and method of proving it are planned first, then clues placed earlier. Do not put every answer on the page or present conclusions without evidence.

| Backward step | Author's working answer | Earlier setup or payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. End-state truth** | Rocket's rare Ditto uses **Polymorph** to transform abducted humans into **specific ordered Pokémon species with essentially perfect stats**, sold on the black market. Human memories fade and eventually the transformation becomes irreversible | Act IV reveal and Act V intervention |
| **2. What constitutes proof?** | An **early-stage victim's mixed human/Pokémon DNA** demonstrates the transformation process. The **unusual birthmark retained by permanently transformed Darrow** points to his identity but is not conclusive proof alone; corroboration may come from family knowledge and records. Evidence must connect League staff, abductions, species orders, and shipments | Late Act IV examination and later corroboration; records and evidence recovered in Act V |
| **3. Personal confirmation** | The starter, whatever its chosen species, was **Darrow, 32**, a stubborn but kindhearted odd-job worker helping his widowed sister raise her children. He was captured near a restricted League area while searching for Herdier, then transformed via Polymorph. **Only Chikorita-Darrow escapes by using Vine Whip on a lock** and is recaptured in the opening; **Charmander/Piplup-Darrow remains captive until the ship transfer** | Scene 11: the woman and children inspire affection, and the normally Pokémon-averse Herdier is delighted to see the starter; the starter is reluctant to leave. Independently identified in scenes 20–21 |
| **4. Early anomalies** | Cages and misleading shipment procedures contradict routine relocation; Cassius has avoided asking questions; the companion's behavior is a little unusual | Scenes 1–5 and 9–12 |
| **5. Misleading but fair interpretations** | At first it seems like illegal Pokémon trafficking, poor husbandry, or unusual breeding rather than human transformation | Protagonist and player should be able to interpret events plausibly before the reveal |
| **6. Consequence and resolution** | The evidence must allow an actual intervention, not just an exposition scene | Information, relationships, and access determine the shape of the return and rescue |

**Open questions to settle before drafting revealing scenes:** Darrow's sister's name and family details; the exact shape and placement of Darrow's birthmark; which surviving documents or witnesses corroborate that clue; Rocket's operational timeline and the timing of the next black-market delivery; and which other victims can still be restored. **The opening capture is always Chikorita, but only captures Darrow in the Chikorita-starter route.**

## Interactive-fiction approach

- Preserve the five-act spine while letting choices alter tone, relationships, chosen starter behavior, optional job scenes, available assistance, and final rescue outcomes.
- Internal thoughts can reflect a chosen emotional interpretation without rerouting every major event.
- Scene 11 should point toward the correct answer in hindsight but **not** allow a knowledgeable character to state the secret prematurely.
- Cassius must not become magically helpful or omniscient. He can risk his livelihood and family stability for a specific, limited act of assistance.
- Do not lock the player out of the main story for missing a minor clue.
- Keep jobs narrative rather than repetitive resource grinding; **no numerical credits**.
- No prose chapters, engine changes, sprite assets, or world map beyond the opening geography are authorized by this outline alone.
